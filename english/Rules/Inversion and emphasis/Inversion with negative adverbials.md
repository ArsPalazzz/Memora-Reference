
## What is an inversion with a negative adverbial?

In formal English, we can use a **negative or restrictive adverb** at the beginning of a sentence to make it more emphatic or dramatic. When we do this, the adverb is then followed by an inversion: **auxiliary verb + subject (+ verb)**. See the example below:

- _I could find my keys **nowhere**. ⇒_ _**Nowhere** **could I find** my keys._ 

When there is no auxiliary verb, we use **do**/**does** (present) or **did** (past) as auxiliary.

- _I understand the true meaning **only now**. ⇒ **Only now** **do I understand** the true meaning._ 
- _I did**n’t** say anything **until she arrived**. ⇒_ _**Not until she arrived** **did I say** anything._

#### Hardly, barely, scarcely, no sooner

We normally use **hardly … when, barely … when**, **scarcely … when**, and **no sooner … than** in narratives with past perfect in the inversion.  
  
We use **no sooner … than** (NOT **that**, or **when**)

#### Adverbial + clause + inversion

❗ **Note** that after some adverbials, such as **only if** and **only when** we use a subordinate clause (subject + verb), and that the inversion is never in the subordinate clause but in the main verb of the sentence. Check the sentence below:

- _**Only when do I sleep** **I can forget** about the accident. ![](https://test-english.com/staging11/wp-content/uploads/wrong.png)_ 
- _**Only when I sleep** **can I forget** about the accident. ![](https://test-english.com/staging11/wp-content/uploads/correct.png)_ 

The same happens with other adverbials, such as **not since** and **not until**; we use a subordinate clause (subject + verb), and the inversion is never in the subordinate clause but in the sentence’s main verb. Check the sentence below:

- _**Not since was I a child** **I have had** such a great time. ![](https://test-english.com/staging11/wp-content/uploads/wrong.png)_  
- _**Not since I was a child** **have** **I had** such a great time__. ![](https://test-english.com/staging11/wp-content/uploads/correct.png)_ 

If you are in doubt, it’s always useful to look at the sentence without the negative adverbial at the beginning.

- _**I can forget** about the accident only when I sleep._ 
- _**I haven’t had** such a great time since I was a child._

The main subject and verb are at the beginning of the sentence, and this is the element that must be in the inversion.

#### Not

We must always use **not** followed by another element before the inversion.

- _**Not** **can we see** such great expressions of art **often**. ![](https://test-english.com/staging11/wp-content/uploads/wrong.png)_ 
- _**Not often** **can we see** such great expressions of art._ _![](https://test-english.com/staging11/wp-content/uploads/correct.png)_

#### Adverbs of frequency

We can also use inversion after the negative or restrictive adverbs of frequency, such as **seldom**, **rarely**, or **never**.  
  
We often use **never** to talk about experiences. In that case, we normally use present perfect or past perfect.

## Разговорная инверсия: no way would I ever

То же правило, но в **живой речи** — не только в формальном английском.

> **No way would I ever** agree to that.

Обычный порядок: *I would never agree.*  
С **No way** в начале → **would I** (инверсия):

| Обычно | С инверсией |
|---|---|
| I would never do that. | **No way would I ever** do that. |
| I would never have guessed. | **Never would I have** guessed. |

**No way** = «ни за что» (разговорно). **would I** — не вопрос, а **эмоциональный отказ**.
